## Motivation

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) have become the standard tool for reverse-engineering what transformers represent, decomposing dense activations into sparse, human-interpretable features. Over the past year the technique jumped from single-modality LLMs to **vision–language** models — SAE-V (Lou et al., 2025, ICML, [arXiv:2502.17514](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.17514)), Zhang et al. (2024, [arXiv:2411.14982](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.14982)), and Pach et al. (2025, [arXiv:2504.02821](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.02821)), alongside crosscoders for cross-model feature comparison (Lindsey et al., 2024). Yet a whole class of multimodal models has been left untouched: **biology–language models**, where a protein or DNA foundation model is fused into an LLM. No published work applies SAEs to a biology–LLM fusion model. Tellingly, the recent bio-SAE field review (Orlov et al., 2026) lists *"scaling SAE analysis to multimodal architectures"* among its top field priorities — but frames it entirely as a vision problem and never considers biology–language fusion. That is the gap we are the first to enter, and it is a timely one: a wave of bio-LLM models has appeared, and how they integrate biological sequence with natural language is essentially unknown.

## The setting: biology–language fusion models

Several models now graft a biological foundation model onto an LLM, each with a different fusion strategy: **BioReason** projects Evo 2 (DNA) embeddings into Qwen3-4B; **BioReason-Pro** fuses ESM3 protein embeddings (plus a GO-graph encoder) into Qwen; and Peter's vocab-integration model adds a DNA tokenizer with a mixture-of-experts head on Nemotron-3-Super. We do not understand how any of them combine the two modalities. The interpretability questions are concrete and biologically meaningful: *which biological (DNA/protein) features actually carry the task signal? Does the LLM align biological content with text concepts, or keep them separate? And how do different fusion architectures compare on common interpretability ground?* Because the SAE methodology transfers directly from vision-language MLLMs — hook the LLM residual stream, train an SAE, interpret the features — we can bring the same lens to biology, and use it to compare fusion designs head-to-head.

Underneath these sits a higher-stakes question with a direct bearing on strategy: are these fusion models genuinely *integrating* biology and language — learning something neither unimodal model could do alone — or are they effectively doing sophisticated **tool-calling**, where the LLM consumes the bio encoder's output like a lookup result without deeply integrating it? That distinction decides whether multimodal bio-LLMs are worth investing in over a cheaper retrieval-or-tool pipeline. SAE interpretability gives direct evidence: genuine cross-modal features would demonstrate real integration and justify the multimodal bet; their absence — or shallow, shortcut-like use (for instance, answering a variant-effect question from the gene name rather than the sequence) — would show the fusion is doing less than it appears and point to what a more integrated model would need. Either outcome yields evidence about *whether and how* investing in multimodal biology models pays off, not just a description of one model's internals.

## What we have established so far

Working on **BioReason-Pro (ESM3 + Qwen)** at layer 16, we have learned three things: how to *train* SAEs on multimodal-bio activations, that the model does learn *high-quality reasoning features*, and — most importantly — *how these models fuse (and don't) at the feature level*. (BioReason-Pro also carries a GO-graph modality; for this analysis we drop the GO tokens and focus on protein↔text fusion, since those tokens are a fixed ontology reduction rather than per-protein signal.)

**Multimodal SAEs demand modality-aware training.** The two modalities occupy radically different magnitude regimes: protein-token residuals carry roughly **50× the norm of text tokens** (median ‖·‖ ≈ 2287 vs ≈ 45). Without per-token input normalization the reconstruction objective — and hence the learned dictionary — is dominated by the loud biological tokens. The more consequential lever is **modality loss-balancing**. Protein tokens are a small minority of the stream, so a naively trained SAE starves them. Rebalancing the training mix to **~50/50 protein:text tokens** (from the raw ~15% protein / ~80% text), the count of *protein-selective* features at layer 16 rises from **98 to 1,840** — nearly a 19× increase — while text-selective features fall from 20,476 to 13,739. The effect is a rebalancing of capacity: the SAE devotes real dictionary space to the starved protein modality, at a modest cost on the text side (which stays plentiful — still ~590 high-quality, low-frequency text features), rather than learning a text-dominated dictionary. (Balancing does not make individual text features *higher-quality* — it redistributes capacity toward the minority modality.)

We focus on **layer 16** here (flagged by our earlier per-layer analysis as a strong layer for interpretable features); *how* features — especially reasoning features — evolve across depth is itself a question we want to study (see next steps).

**The model learns clean, specific reasoning features.** Using an auto-interpretation pipeline we built for the multimodal setting — which labels each feature separately on its protein/DNA/reasoning/answer token bands and flags concepts shared across bands — we already surface crisp, rare (high-quality) features in the model's *reasoning*. Examples: **F12493** encodes *cell junctions* — it fires on "junction" consistently across the reasoning, answer, and prompt, and the proteins in those samples are enriched for the *cell junction* GO term at **p≈4e-25** (synapse, p≈1e-15); **F7856** encodes *embryonic development* (0.09% firing rate; samples enriched for developmental GO terms); **F6217** encodes *establishment of localization / transport* (0.02% firing; samples enriched for *establishment of localization*, p≈1e-17). These are interpretable, functionally-coherent, and *non-trivial* — F12493 is not merely reading a printed GO term; it tracks a concept that recurs across the model's own reasoning and lands on exactly the proteins that concept applies to. Recovering interpretable reasoning features is itself a high-visibility direction: Goodfire's recent study of DeepSeek-R1 (Hazra et al., 2025) drew wide attention for surfacing reasoning features (backtracking, arithmetic operations) — yet with a *standard* SAE trained on a single residual-stream layer, the same approach we use, with nothing reasoning-specific about the method. The novelty here is applying it to a *biology* reasoning model and tying the reasoning features to the underlying protein/DNA function.

We are candid about the pipeline's current limits, too. The labeler keys on each feature's single peak-activation token, which can miss **recurring multi-token phrases**: e.g. a feature that fires on "The" at the start of protein domain-architecture sentences ("…from the linear domain architecture. **The** N-terminus…") is currently labeled "syntactic," when its real, consistent context is *domain-architecture description*. Surfacing recurring n-grams/context across a feature's windows (rather than only the peak token) is a concrete near-term improvement.

**A promising hypothesis (early, not yet settled): cross-modal features may require an aligned representation.** In the layer-16 analysis so far, we have not found features that cleanly bind a biological concept to its text — beyond structural boundary markers — either across the full 117k-protein set or in a modality-balanced DNA control. We deliberately do *not* treat this as a definitive result yet; it is an early observation, and it points to a specific, testable hypothesis about *why*. SAE-V's cross-modal score is a cosine over the *raw hidden states* of a feature's top tokens (the SAE only selects and ranks which tokens to compare), so it inherits the geometry of the raw residual stream — and in BioReason-Pro the protein and text representations sit in **near-orthogonal** subspaces (mean cross-modal cosine ≈ 0; DNA a modest 0.37), where such a metric reads ≈0 almost by construction. This *may* be a property of the encoder rather than the method: SAE-V worked on LLaVA/Chameleon, whose vision encoders are text-aligned (CLIP is image-text contrastive; Chameleon shares a token space), whereas ESM3 and Evo 2 have never seen text — we project their embeddings into the LLM's space, but sharing a dimensionality is not the same as sharing directions. The hypothesis, then, is that recovering cross-modal SAE features requires a representation in which the two modalities are directionally aligned. **This is precisely the experiment we want to run next** (§ next steps): if cross-modal features appear in a contrastively-aligned bio-LLM but not in BioReason-Pro, the hypothesis is supported.

_(A methodological aside, deliberately kept minor for now: we also built an **enrichment-grounded** way to interpret protein features — for the proteins a feature fires on, we run a Fisher over-representation test against GO/UniProt annotations, yielding a statistical label with a p-value rather than an LLM guess. This is promising as a method, but the specific protein-feature interpretations are **preliminary and under verification** — many protein features are weak or high-frequency, and the enrichment can mislead if read without checking a feature's frequency and its actual activating windows. We treat these labels as candidates, not conclusions, until validated.)_

## Contributions

Taken together, the work offers (1) the first SAE analysis of a biology–LLM fusion model, with an open-source extract→train→interpret recipe; (2) a modality-aware training recipe (normalization + loss-balancing) that makes both modalities' features learnable; (3) an early finding and a testable hypothesis — we do not yet see feature-level cross-modal fusion in current projector-based bio-LLMs, plausibly because cosine-based cross-modal metrics require encoder–text alignment, together with the experiment that would confirm or refute it; and (4) a proposal for a new, modality-aware SAE architecture (next section).

## A modality-aware SAE architecture (proposed)

Our balanced-loss and Matryoshka experiments were loss-level patches on a single shared dictionary: they improved feature *utilization* and modality separation but not feature *quality*. The likely reason is structural — different magnitude regimes and near-orthogonal subspaces make one shared dictionary with one normalization a poor fit, producing the magnitude-domination and minority-modality starvation we measured. Building on the Matryoshka-SAE line of work (Bussmann et al., 2025 — *confirm citation*) and group-sparse SAEs (MGSAE — *confirm citation*), we see room to design a *multimodal-specific* SAE for higher-quality features. A natural candidate is a **mixture-of-experts SAE**, with a few design choices that matter:

- **Modality-specific experts** (protein / text, and GO if reintroduced), each with its own normalization and dictionary — and **its own TopK** tuned to that modality's natural sparsity (protein may need K≈64 to reconstruct well, text K≈32; a single global TopK would just default to the majority modality).
- **A fusion expert that *competes* for tokens rather than mopping up residuals.** Under soft routing with a modality-prior bias (initialized toward the correct expert but free to deviate), a genuinely cross-modal token — e.g. a protein residue being integrated with text mid-reasoning — could route *primarily* to the fusion expert. A fusion expert that only ever sees leftovers can never win a truly cross-modal token, so it would only ever learn a small correction.
- **A cross-modal training signal for the fusion expert.** Trained on reconstruction residuals alone it becomes a garbage collector for whatever the modality experts miss (often noise). A group-sparse / cross-modal-consistency objective — tying the fusion activations of semantically related tokens across modalities — is what would make it *measure* integration rather than absorb noise.

A sensible **first step is to skip the fusion expert**: train only the modality-specific experts, measure the per-modality residual (unexplained variance), and test whether those residuals correlate *across* modalities. If they do, a fusion expert would have real cross-modal signal to capture; if not, it would be a garbage collector. This is best attempted alongside the Prot2Text-V2 control (below), which can independently confirm whether any cross-modal signal exists to find.


## Next steps

The highest-value experiment is a direct test of the alignment precondition. Prot2Text-V2 ([`xiao-fei/Prot2Text-V2-11B-Instruct-hf`](https://huggingface.co/xiao-fei/Prot2Text-V2-11B-Instruct-hf), [arXiv:2505.11194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11194)) is architecturally matched to BioReason — ESM2-3B → adapter → LLaMA-3.1-8B — but adds a CLIP-style contrastive alignment (H-SCALE) between protein and text. It is the ideal control: same modalities, with alignment as the only variable. If cross-modal features appear there but not in BioReason-Pro, the alignment hypothesis is confirmed; the recipe is just a thin extractor on top of our existing pipeline. In parallel we are standing up two **baselines** to validate the methodology itself — replicating SAE-V on LLaVA-Next (to confirm our cross-modal metric reproduces their known positive result, ruling out a pipeline bug) and analyzing BioReason-DNA (does DNA, whose representation is less orthogonal to text, fuse differently than protein?).

Two further directions follow. First, **structural grounding of biological features**: because we know both the proteins a feature fires on and the specific residues, we can map those residues onto 3D structure and ask whether they cluster in a functional site (e.g. a catalytic pocket). This is a natural fit for the **AlphaFold-2 / OpenFold NIMs** — fetch or fold the relevant structures and overlay a feature's firing residues — turning a sequence-level label into a mechanistic, structural one, and a compelling figure. We are also setting up causal checks (patching biological-token activations into a text-only pass on a small set of curated examples) to see whether the fusion signal is causal; and prototyping the MoE-SAE above. Two more directions we find especially promising: a **depth study of reasoning features** — tracking how reasoning features emerge and sharpen across layers and where biological signal is read into the reasoning (our sweep hints at the mid-20s), which is a *general pattern question for multimodal models*, not just this one; and a **comparison against attention** — identifying which heads and layers actually move biological information into the text stream, the natural test of the attention-mediated-fusion hypothesis and a cross-check on the residual-stream SAE features.
